Dear Hiring Manager,

I am applying for the **Principal Technologist, Office of the CTO** role because it describes the kind of work I already gravitate toward: walk in, understand what is broken, scope something **small enough to ship**, and deliver a solution that moves the business—then improve or replace it when the long-term architecture is ready. Your posting’s line about building the **bicycle, not the race car** matches how I have operated in large, regulated-feeling customer environments (retail and omnichannel), where “perfect in six months” is often the enemy of “working next week.”

In recent roles I have **shipped** while staying **hands-on**. At Bath & Body Works I **architected and delivered** the end-to-end **email migration** from SFCC through SFMC APIs, owned **authentication and deliverability** work (DKIM, SPF, DMARC), and **led** a **company-wide** **headless migration** (SiteGenesis → PWA Kit) with explicit **API contracts** for auth, loyalty, and orders across **US and Canada**. I also **directed** the nationwide **BOPIS** rollout across **114** stores—exactly the sort of cross-functional, operations-heavy program where success is measured by whether stores and customers can rely on it, not by slide count. Earlier, as a **Business Systems Analyst** at Columbia Sportswear, I lived in the space between **Product, pricing, promotions, and engineering**, aligning stakeholders and third-party integrations so initiatives actually landed. I am comfortable being the person people **come to first** when they need a pragmatic path, and I know how to **pull in** the right engineers, vendors, or partners when the answer is bigger than one person.

I am not a career project manager hiding behind technical vocabulary—I build **integrations, automations, and workflows**, and I use **AI and LLM tools** daily to speed research, draft specs, and automate repetitive engineering tasks, always with human review where it matters. I also know how to be the **counterweight** to scope creep: interim solutions should be **easy to replace**, bounded in dependencies, and honest about what “good enough for now” means so it does not silently become permanent debt.

Clover’s mission—**Medicare done differently**—is a compelling reason to bring that pattern into healthcare. I will be direct: I have not spent a career inside **HIPAA**-labeled systems, but I have spent years on **high-stakes customer data paths**, production incidents, and cross-org delivery where trust and rigor matter. I learn domains quickly when paired with strong engineering and clinical partners, and I would welcome the chance to do that at Clover.

Thank you for considering my application. A tailored CV for this role is available with my materials; I would welcome a conversation with the OCTO team about how I could help **free leadership’s focus** by owning tactical problems end-to-end.

Sincerely,
